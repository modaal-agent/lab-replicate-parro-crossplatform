1. **One app, four tabs** — Home, Calendar, Chat, Settings — with static placeholder data, built
   twice: `native-swift/` in SwiftUI with the Xcode project generated by XcodeGen, and
   `ionic-capacitor/` with Ionic 9 and Capacitor 8 (§2–§4).
2. **The phone shells come first** and use each stack's standard containers only: SwiftUI `TabView`
   with `Tab` and a `NavigationStack` per tab; Ionic `ion-tabs` with `ion-router-outlet`. Neither
   build draws a custom tab bar (§3.2, §4.3).
3. **The iPad idiom for tabs-to-sidebar and list-next-to-detail is in both installed SDKs.**
   `TabView` with `.tabViewStyle(.sidebarAdaptable)` (iOS 18) shows a sidebar on a regular-width
   window, and `NavigationSplitView` (iOS 16) shows a list next to its detail. Apple's talk 111461
   states that both "are fully adaptive across every pose" of iPhone Duo (§1.4, §1.5).
4. **`ArrangementView` is in neither installed SDK.** Apple ships it with iOS 27.1, and Xcode 27.1
   beta is listed as "coming later this month" on Apple's iPhone Duo page. It places two content
   views inside a `NavigationStack`, and talk 111463 says not to put `NavigationSplitView` inside
   one. The master-detail pages therefore use `NavigationSplitView`; `ArrangementView` is tried in
   phase 5 on the Calendar screen, which has two panes and no navigation between them (D3).
5. **The Liquid Glass tab bar takes no code in SwiftUI**: a `TabView` built with the iOS 26 SDK
   draws it. Ionic 9 ships no Liquid Glass theme, and `ion-tab-bar` is HTML inside the web view
   (§1.4, §1.6, D8).
6. **Node 20.19.5 is installed; Capacitor 8 requires Node 22 or later.** Phase 2 starts with a Node
   upgrade on this machine (§1.3).
7. **Six phases** (§6): this spec, the two phone shells, the two wide layouts, then iPhone Duo on
   Xcode 27.1. Each phase appends its measurements to §5.

### 1.5 iPhone Duo: what Apple has published

Read through WebFetch's summary of each page. Before a phase depends on an exact API spelling, re-read
the transcript at the timestamp given.

- **[developer.apple.com/iphone-duo](https://developer.apple.com/iphone-duo/)** lists Xcode 27.1 beta
  and the "Preparing your app for iPhone Duo" documentation as "coming later this month". The page
  gives no device release date and no display dimensions.
- **Talk 111461, "Prepare your app for iPhone Duo":**
  - The outer display behaves like other iPhone models. The inner display is regular in both size
    classes and does not honor supported interface orientations; the talk says to lay out by size
    class, not by orientation.
  - "NavigationSplitView, UISplitViewController, TabView, and UITabBarController are fully adaptive
    across every pose — columns collapse when closed and tile or overlay when open."
  - At 5:44 it shows `TabView { … }.defaultTabBarPlacement(.sidebar)` and, in UIKit,
    `tabBarController.sidebar.preferredPlacement = .sidebar`.
  - With the iOS 27 SDK the app extends left of the status bar on the inner display; with the iOS
    27.1 SDK it reaches the screen edge and lays standard navigation and toolbar buttons out
    vertically.
  - Testing is in the iPhone Duo simulator in Device Hub, in Xcode 27.1, which has controls to open,
    close, rotate and fold the device.
- **Talk 111463, "Strike a pose with adaptive layouts on iPhone Duo":**
  - `ArrangementView` takes a primary and a secondary view; `.arrangementViewStyle(.split)` lays
    them out like an `HStack`/`VStack` and `.arrangementViewStyle(.overlay)` like a `ZStack`. The
    UIKit equivalent is `UIArrangementViewController`.
  - At 9:20: system-provided arrangements are available from iOS 27.1.
  - "ArrangementViews don't provide navigation infrastructure, so avoid putting navigation
    containers like NavigationSplitView inside one."
  - Reserved regions are read with `proxy.reservedRegions(kind:)`: `.division` is the fold, with
    zero width when the device is flat; `.occlusion` is the camera.
- **Talk 111462, "Raise the bar with iPhone Duo":** bars move to a vertical axis on the inner
  display. It shows `toolbarVerticalCompressionBehavior(.prefersToolbarItems)` on a `Tab`'s content
  and `toolbarVerticalBehavior(.disabled)` to keep bars horizontal.

## 7. Decisions

**Added 2026-09-13:** §10.1 confirms D1–D8 and amends D9.

**Added 2026-09-13:** §13.6 amends D6 and D8 for `ionic-capacitor/` (D15), and §13.10 O8 amends D1:
the two Capacitor variants take their own bundle identifiers.

- **D1 — Product name `TabShell`, bundle identifier `dev.modaal.lab.tabshell`, in both builds.** The
  app is named for what it is, and the product identifiers carry no third-party brand.
- **D2 — iPhone-only in phase 1, iPhone and iPad from phase 3.** The reference sets
  `TARGETED_DEVICE_FAMILY: '1'` (§1.7). The iPad sidebar and the split view need the iPad idiom;
  an iPhone-only app runs on iPad at iPhone size. Making the switch in phase 3 puts it inside the
  change §5 measures.
- **D3 — `NavigationSplitView` for list and detail; `ArrangementView` only in phase 5, only for
  Calendar.** `NavigationSplitView` is available from iOS 16 in both installed SDKs, and Apple
  states it adapts to every iPhone Duo pose (§1.4, §1.5). `ArrangementView` is absent from both
  SDKs, needs iOS 27.1, and must not wrap a `NavigationSplitView` (§1.5). Calendar's week strip and
  agenda are two content panes with no navigation between them, which is the case talk 111463
  describes.
- **D4 — Deployment target iOS 26.0 for `native-swift/`.** The Liquid Glass bar and
  `tabBarMinimizeBehavior` need iOS 26.0; `Tab` and `.sidebarAdaptable` need iOS 18.0 (§1.4). A 26.0
  floor removes `if #available` branches around glass. Cost: the installed iOS 18.6 runtime is not
  tested. `ionic-capacitor/` keeps Capacitor's template value, 15.0 (§1.3).
- **D5 — Ionic binding: React, pending §9 O1.** Ionic 9 rebuilt its React Router 6 integration to
  retain views (§1.6), which the "state across a resize" measure in §5 exercises. Cost:
  `react-router` in the dependency list. Angular 18–22 and Vue 3.5 are the alternatives Ionic 9
  supports.
- **D6 — Minimal dependencies.** `native-swift/`: no Swift packages. `ionic-capacitor/`: the tabs
  starter's packages minus unused Capacitor plugins. `stay-liquid` is not adopted in phase 2: it
  installs from a GitHub URL, renders only the tab bar, and does not document an iPad sidebar
  (§1.6).
- **D7 — System navigation bar in SwiftUI.** The screenshots show a custom header (§1.2). The system
  bar is what the iOS 27.1 SDK lays out vertically on the inner display (§1.5); a custom header
  would need that layout written by hand. Cost: the header differs from the screenshot, and phase 1
  records the difference under fidelity.
- **D8 — System Liquid Glass tab bar in SwiftUI; the flat bar in CSS in Ionic.** A system `TabView`
  on the iOS 26 SDK draws the glass bar; matching the flat bar in §1.2 would take a custom bar in
  place of `TabView`'s, which loses the sidebar form phase 3 depends on. `ion-tab-bar` is styled
  with CSS and can match §1.2. Both builds use their stack's default, and the tab bar difference is recorded
  under fidelity in phases 1 and 2.
- **D9 — Xcode 26.6 for phases 1–4, Xcode 27.1 beta for phase 5.** 26.6 is the selected Xcode and
  meets Capacitor 8's 26.0 minimum. Cost: `defaultTabBarPlacement(_:)` and `TabRole.prominent`
  (iOS 27.0, §1.4) are not available before phase 5.

| option | how | costs |

### 13.5 Costs of carrying two variants under option A

- Each measurement or screenshot round in phase 2, and in each later phase that covers both variants
  (§13.9 O7), runs one build, one `cap sync` and one install per variant.
- `matched` adds one npm package. `stock`'s bundle leaves it out, and `npm ci` still installs it
  (D15).
- `stock`'s list markup includes the `IonItemGroup` level the theme requires (D16).
- The theme's selectors target Ionic's internal classes, and its peer range ends below `@ionic/core`
  10. An Ionic major upgrade can break `matched` while `stock` still builds.
- The tab bar in the conversation differs between the variants (D14), so phase 4's "state across a
  resize" check (§5) runs in both.
- On one simulator the three builds replace each other while they share D1's bundle identifier
  (§13.9 O8).

### 16.4 Estimates

- **Hand-written lines for the native look.**
  - `native-swift/` has 1,605 hand-written lines; `ionic-capacitor/` with both variants has 2,692, 1.7 times
    as many.
  - About 1,000 of the 2,692 exist only for `matched`: the 754 lines of `src/styles/matched/` and
    `variant.ts`, and the 240 lines pass 2b added to shared TSX, of which 80 replaced lines of pass 2a.
  - Basis: §16.1. The estimate treats `src/` at `93ad230`, 1,844 lines of which 33 are in the starter's files
    (§14.2), as the cost of the screens and their behaviour, and pass 2b's lines as the cost of the look.
- **Scaffolded share of `ionic-capacitor/`.**
  - About a quarter of the committed text lines excluding lock files: 1,071 of 3,763.
  - About half of the committed files: 37 of 73 were generated and not edited by hand.
  - The generated files outside `src/` are the starter's build, lint, test and web entry files (12
    unchanged, 5 edited) and the 20 files of the iOS template (17 unchanged, 3 edited by tools only).
  - Of the app's own code in `src/`, the starter supplies 34 unchanged lines (`main.tsx`, `setupTests.ts`,
    `App.test.tsx`, `vite-env.d.ts`) and the imports at the top of `App.tsx`. Every screen, the fixture, the
    model and all styling are written by hand.
  - `native-swift/` has no committed generated files. XcodeGen generates 455 uncommitted lines from its
    47-line `xcodegen.yml`.
- **Time and tool calls for the native look.**
  - `ionic-capacitor/` took pass 2a and pass 2b: 343 tool calls, 112 minutes of active time, and 8 screenshot
    rounds in pass 2b alone.
  - For `native-swift/` the transcript gives only the chat detail round, 58 tool calls in 19 minutes, and
    DISCOVERY.md gives 6 rounds for phase 1.
  - Basis: §16.3. Phase 1's missing transcript entries prevent a count for all of `native-swift/`, so this
    comparison rests on rounds and lines rather than on time.
- **Dependence on `native-swift/`.**
  - Pass 2b took every own value in `index.css` from `native-swift/`'s screenshots, Swift sources or
    accessibility frames, and compared each round against its screenshots.
  - Building `matched` without `native-swift/` in the repository would need these values from another
    source: Apple's design resources, or measurements of iOS system apps. This spec does not estimate that
    effort.
- **Upkeep.**
  - `matched` rests on the theme, whose peer range ends below `@ionic/core` 10 (§13.5), and on the Ionic
    internals listed in §16.2.
  - An Ionic or theme upgrade that changes one of them needs a new screenshot round in `matched`, and
    `stock` does not.
  - `native-swift/` has no third-party UI dependency. An iOS SDK change reaches it through SwiftUI, as the v1
    to v5 workarounds did in phase 1.
  - This estimate is not measured; phase 4 (wide layout) and phase 6 (dark mode) change both variants and can
    measure it.
- **Fidelity reached.**
  - `matched` has 5 gaps against `native-swift/` (§15.6): SF Symbols, refraction, the Calendar title's
    transition, the time format, and a 4 pt sliver in the selected-day scroll.
  - SF Symbols and refraction are not available to web content, and the other three are fixable in TSX or
    CSS.
